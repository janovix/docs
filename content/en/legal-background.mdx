# Legal Background: LFPIORPI

## What is the LFPIORPI?

The **Ley Federal para la Prevención e Identificación de Operaciones con Recursos de Procedencia Ilícita** (LFPIORPI) is Mexico's primary anti-money laundering legislation. Originally published in the _Diario Oficial de la Federación_ on October 17, 2012, it has been most recently reformed on **July 16, 2025**.

The law establishes a framework requiring businesses in specific sectors — known as _sujetos obligados_ (obligated subjects) — to identify their clients, record transactions above monetary thresholds, and file reports with the _Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público_ (SHCP).

**Legal citation:** Ley Federal para la Prevención e Identificación de Operaciones con Recursos de Procedencia Ilícita, D.O.F. 17-10-2012, última reforma D.O.F. 16-07-2025.

## Purpose of the Law (Art. 1–2)

> _"El objeto de esta Ley es proteger el sistema financiero y la economía nacional, estableciendo medidas y procedimientos para prevenir y detectar actos u operaciones que involucren recursos de procedencia ilícita…"_ — Art. 2

The law has two primary objectives:

1. **Protect the financial system and national economy** by preventing illicit funds from entering legitimate commerce
2. **Gather intelligence** useful for investigating and prosecuting money-laundering crimes (_Delitos de Operaciones con Recursos de Procedencia Ilícita_) and dismantling the financial structures of criminal organizations

## Key Definitions (Art. 3)

| Term                                     | Definition                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Actividades Vulnerables**              | Activities carried out by financial entities under Art. 14 and the **20** activities listed in Art. 17 that are subject to identification, monitoring, and reporting obligations |
| **Avisos**                               | Reports that obligated subjects must file with the SHCP when transactions exceed the notification threshold                                                                      |
| **Beneficiario Controlador**             | The ultimate beneficial owner — the physical person(s) who ultimately benefit from or exercise effective control over a legal entity that is a client                            |
| **Cliente o Usuaria**                    | Any physical or moral person, or trust, that conducts acts or operations with an obligated subject                                                                               |
| **Persona Políticamente Expuesta (PEP)** | A physical person who holds or has held public office in Mexico or a foreign country, and their close associates (added by the 2025 reform)                                      |
| **UMA**                                  | Unidad de Medida y Actualización — the monetary unit used for threshold calculations, updated annually by INEGI                                                                  |
| **Entidades Colegiadas**                 | Recognized professional associations that can assist obligated subjects in fulfilling their obligations                                                                          |
| **Riesgo**                               | The probability that vulnerable activities could be used to launder money or finance criminal organizations (added 2025)                                                         |

<Callout type="info">
	**UMA value for 2026:** The daily UMA value is **$117.31 MXN** (INEGI,
	effective February 1, 2026). All thresholds in this document are expressed in
	UMA multiples; multiply by the daily UMA in force for the calendar year to
	approximate pesos.
</Callout>

## Governing Authorities (Art. 5–12)

### Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público (SHCP)

The SHCP is the competent administrative authority. Its key powers include:

- Receiving _avisos_ from obligated subjects
- Maintaining the registry of obligated subjects
- Issuing general rules and regulations
- Conducting verification visits
- Imposing administrative sanctions
- Establishing risk-specific measures for high-risk countries

### Fiscalía General de la República – Unidad Especializada

A specialized financial analysis unit within the Federal Prosecutor's office, focused on investigating money laundering crimes. Has direct access to aviso databases and can request information from the SHCP.

## Vulnerable Activities and Thresholds (Art. 17)

The following activities trigger **identification obligations** when the transaction meets the identification threshold. They trigger a **mandatory aviso (notice) obligation** when the notice threshold is met.

<Callout type="warning">
	Accumulated transactions with the same client over a **6-month period** that
	collectively exceed the aviso threshold are treated as a single notifiable
	transaction.
</Callout>

## Obligations of Obligated Subjects (Art. 18)

All businesses subject to Art. 17 must comply with the following obligations:

<Tabs items={['Client Identification', 'Record Keeping', 'Reporting', 'Internal Controls']}>
<Tabs.Tab>
**I. Client Identification (KYC)**
Directly identify and verify the identity of every client using official identification documents. Collect copies of all identification.

**II. Business Relationship Information**
For ongoing business relationships: collect information about the client's occupation or business activity (RFC registration data).

**III. Beneficial Controllers**

- For **moral persons and trusts**: obtain documents identifying the _Beneficiario Controlador_ (ultimate beneficial owner)
- For **physical persons**: obtain a declaration as to whether a beneficial controller exists, and if so, their identification

**IV Bis. Registry Registration**
Register as an obligated subject in the SAT's _Padrón de Actividades Vulnerables_ via the online portal.

</Tabs.Tab>
<Tabs.Tab>
**IV. Document Preservation**
Preserve all supporting documentation for vulnerable activities — transaction records, correspondence, analysis — in physical or electronic form at the address registered with the SHCP.

**Retention period: minimum 10 years** from the date of the operation. The period is suspended if litigation is initiated and restarts when a final resolution is issued.

This includes:

- Client identification documents
- Transaction records (sufficient for reconstruction)
- Commercial correspondence related to transactions
- Prior risk analysis performed

</Tabs.Tab>
<Tabs.Tab>
**VI. Filing Avisos**
File notices with the SHCP as required by Art. 17 thresholds.

**Urgent reporting (24 hours):** If there is a suspicion or facts/indications that funds may be linked to money laundering, an aviso must be filed within **24 hours** — even if the transaction was not completed.

**VII. Risk-Based Evaluation** (added 2025)
Conduct a risk assessment to identify, analyze, and mitigate the money laundering risks of your activities and clients.

**VIII. Internal Policies Manual**
Develop and maintain an internal policies manual covering AML procedures, PEP identification, and compliance processes.

</Tabs.Tab>
<Tabs.Tab>
**IX. Personnel Training**
Annual training programs for management, compliance officers, and client-facing employees covering the LFPIORPI and internal procedures.

**X. Automated Monitoring** (added 2025)
Implement automated mechanisms to permanently monitor client transactions for:

- Transactions outside the client's transactional profile
- Accumulated amounts approaching thresholds
- Intensified monitoring for PEPs and high-risk clients

**XI. Internal/External Audit** (added 2025)
Annual audit of AML compliance effectiveness:

- Low/medium risk: internal audit or independent external auditor
- High risk: independent external auditor only

**V. Verification Visits**
Facilitate and cooperate with SHCP verification visits.

</Tabs.Tab>
</Tabs>

## Notice Filing Requirements (Art. 20–26)

### Who Must File

Every obligated subject carrying out a transaction at or above the applicable notice threshold in Art. 17.

### Deadlines

- **Regular avisos**: Filed within the legal timeframe established by the SHCP (generally monthly)
- **Suspicious activity avisos**: Filed within **24 hours** of detection

### Format

Notices must be filed electronically through the SAT portal in the prescribed XML format. Janovix generates this XML automatically.

### Compliance Officer (_Representante Encargada de Cumplimiento_)

Obligated subjects must designate a compliance officer registered with the SHCP who is responsible for filing notices and maintaining compliance.

## Sanctions (Art. 53–65)

Non-compliance is subject to graduated administrative sanctions:

| Violation                       | Fine Range        |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Failure to file an aviso        | 200–2,000 UMAs    |
| Late aviso filing               | 200–2,000 UMAs    |
| Failure to identify clients     | 200–2,000 UMAs    |
| Failure to maintain records     | 200–2,000 UMAs    |
| Repeated or serious violations  | Up to 65,000 UMAs |
| Obstructing verification visits | 500–2,000 UMAs    |

<Callout type="error">
	At the 2026 UMA daily value of **$117.31 MXN**, 65,000 UMAs equals
	approximately **MXN $7.62 million** (order-of-magnitude USD depends on the FX
	rate). Criminal liability may also apply in cases of complicity with money
	laundering.
</Callout>
