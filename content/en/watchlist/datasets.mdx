# Watchlist Datasets

{/* IMAGE: public/images/watchlist/datasets-diagram.png - Diagram showing the 5 data sources, their update cadence, and the hybrid search engine that combines them */}

Janovix screens against five data sources. Understanding each helps you interpret results and manage compliance risk.

## OFAC SDN (Office of Foreign Assets Control)

**Source:** U.S. Department of the Treasury
**Coverage:** Global — individuals, entities, vessels, and aircraft

The OFAC SDN (Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons List) is one of the most comprehensive sanctions lists in the world. Transactions with SDN-listed individuals and entities are prohibited by U.S. law — and increasingly referenced in Mexican AML practice.

### Entry Types

| Type         | Description              |
| ------------ | ------------------------ |
| `individual` | Physical persons         |
| `entity`     | Companies, organizations |
| `vessel`     | Ships                    |
| `aircraft`   | Aircraft                 |

### Data Fields

- Name and aliases (including AKAs and transliterations)
- Nationality and passport numbers
- Date of birth and place of birth
- Known addresses
- Identifiers (passport, national ID, tax ID)
- Programs (the sanction regime, e.g., IRAN, DPRK, SDGT)
- Source lists (SDN, Non-SDN, SSI, CAPTA, etc.)

### Update Cadence

OFAC publishes updates as frequently as daily when new designations occur. Janovix synchronizes OFAC data **daily** via the official SDN XML feed.

## UNSC (United Nations Security Council)

**Source:** United Nations
**Coverage:** Global

The UN Security Council maintains consolidated sanctions lists for specific conflict zones and regimes. These are particularly relevant for international transactions.

### Regimes Covered

- Taliban (AFG)
- Al-Qaida
- DRC (Democratic Republic of Congo)
- Somalia
- Sudan
- Libya
- And others

### Update Cadence

UNSC updates when the Security Council votes to add/remove individuals. Janovix synchronizes **weekly**.

## SAT 69-B (Mexican Tax Authority)

**Source:** Servicio de Administración Tributaria (SAT)
**Coverage:** Mexico-specific

The SAT 69-B list contains Mexican taxpayers (RFC holders) that the SAT has determined issued _Comprobantes Fiscales Digitales_ (CFDI) for non-existent or simulated operations — a key money laundering vehicle.

### SAT 69-B Phases

| Phase                                      | Code                  | Description                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Presumption (_Presuntos_)                  | `PRESUNTO`            | SAT suspects non-existent operations; taxpayer has 15 business days to rebut |
| Rebuttal (_En rebuttal_)                   | `EN_REBATIMIENTO`     | Taxpayer has filed a rebuttal; case under review                             |
| Definitive (_Definitivos_)                 | `DEFINITIVO`          | SAT confirmed non-existent operations; rebuttal period expired               |
| Favorable sentence (_Sentencia favorable_) | `SENTENCIA_FAVORABLE` | Court ruled in taxpayer's favor; removed from definitive list                |

<Callout type="warning">
	Accepting a CFDI from a **Definitivo** SAT 69-B-listed taxpayer as a
	deductible expense can result in tax authority challenges and denial of
	deductions. Screening suppliers against this list is a tax risk management
	best practice beyond AML.
</Callout>

### Update Cadence

The SAT publishes the 69-B list on its website. Janovix synchronizes **weekly** (or on demand via admin ingestion).

## PEP (Politically Exposed Persons)

**Source:** AI-powered database + public sources
**Coverage:** Mexico and international

The LFPIORPI 2025 reform explicitly added PEPs as a defined term (Art. 3, Fraction IX Bis) requiring enhanced due diligence.

A **PEP** is a physical person who holds or has held a prominent public function:

- Presidents, ministers, governors, legislators
- Judges and senior judicial officials
- Military commanders
- Senior executives of state-owned enterprises
- Leaders of political parties
- International organization officials

PEP status also extends to close family members and known associates.

### How PEP Screening Works

PEP searches run on an AI-powered container that:

1. Receives the query name
2. Searches multiple public sources in real time
3. Returns structured results with confidence scores

**Latency:** 10–60 seconds (asynchronous). Results are delivered via SSE.

**Cache:** Results are cached for 24 hours per unique query to reduce latency on repeated searches.

## Adverse Media

**Source:** AI-powered web analysis
**Coverage:** Global

Adverse media screening searches the web and news archives for articles linking the queried individual or entity to:

- Money laundering or financial crimes
- Drug trafficking
- Terrorism financing
- Corruption or bribery
- Fraud or embezzlement

### How Adverse Media Works

1. An AI container receives the query
2. Searches news archives and web sources
3. Analyzes article sentiment and relevance
4. Returns a structured report of relevant findings

**Latency:** 10–60 seconds (asynchronous).

<Callout type="info">
	Adverse media screening is **informational** — a finding doesn't mean the
	person is guilty, only that they have been reported in connection with the
	topic. Use it as a trigger for enhanced due diligence, not as a definitive
	determination.
</Callout>
