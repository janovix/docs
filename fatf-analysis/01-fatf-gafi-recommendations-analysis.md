# FATF/GAFI 40 Recommendations — Analysis Summary

> Source: _Estándares Internacionales sobre la Lucha contra el Lavado de Activos, el Financiamiento del Terrorismo y el Financiamiento de la Proliferación de Armas de Destrucción Masiva — Actualización a diciembre de 2025_ (OCDE/GAFI, translated by GAFILAT).

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The FATF (Financial Action Task Force) — known in Spanish as GAFI (Grupo de Acción Financiera Internacional) — is the intergovernmental body that sets the global standard for combating money laundering (ML/LA), terrorist financing (TF/FT), and proliferation financing (PF/FP). The **40 Recommendations** are accepted by over 200 countries and jurisdictions and form the basis for all national AML/CFT regimes.

The December 2025 edition consolidates all amendments since the 2012 adoption, including significant revisions to:

- **Proportionality and the Risk-Based Approach** (Feb 2025) — countries must actively "allow and encourage" simplified measures for lower-risk areas
- **Payment Transparency / Travel Rule** (Jun 2025) — modernized for ISO 20022 messaging and new payment models
- **Proliferation Financing Sanctions** (Oct 2025) — updated Iran-related UNSC references
- **Asset Recovery** (Oct 2023) — strengthened confiscation, non-conviction-based confiscation, and international cooperation
- **Beneficial Ownership** (Mar 2022 / Feb 2023) — registries required for both legal persons and legal arrangements

The standards are evaluated through **Mutual Evaluations** using a dual methodology: **Technical Compliance** (legal/institutional framework) and **Effectiveness** (11 Immediate Outcomes measuring actual results). Countries undergo 5th-round evaluations under this framework.

### Relevance to Janovix

Janovix is a SaaS compliance platform that enables obligated entities (financial institutions, DNFBPs/APNFDs) to implement the preventive measures required by these Recommendations. ## 2. The 40 Recommendations by Thematic Group

### Group A — AML/CFT/CFP Policies and Coordination (R.1–R.2)

#### R.1 — Risk Assessment and Risk-Based Approach (Evaluación de riesgos y aplicación de un enfoque basado en riesgo)

Countries must identify, assess, and understand their ML/TF/PF risks at the national level, and require financial institutions and DNFBPs to do the same at the entity level. Key principles:

- **Higher risk** → enhanced measures (mandatory)
- **Lower risk** → simplified measures (must be allowed and encouraged)
- **Proportionality** is the core principle: measures must match the level of risk
- PF risk is strictly limited to non-compliance/evasion of R.7 targeted financial sanctions
- Financial institutions and DNFBPs must document risk assessments, keep them updated, and share findings with supervisors

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- Countries must conduct national risk assessments (NRAs) on an ongoing basis
- Results must be shared with all competent authorities, SRBs, FIs, and DNFBPs
- Supervisors must review risk profiles, assessments, and mitigation measures of supervised entities
- Entities may differentiate the scope of measures (e.g., normal CDD for onboarding but enhanced monitoring, or vice versa)

#### R.2 — National Cooperation and Coordination (Cooperación y coordinación nacional)

Countries must have national AML/CFT/CFP policies informed by identified risks, reviewed periodically, with a designated authority or coordination mechanism. Operational cooperation must include FIU, law enforcement, supervisors, and other competent authorities, including compatibility with data protection rules.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- Frameworks must include relevant ministries, law enforcement, FIU, security/intelligence, customs, supervisors, tax authorities, beneficial ownership registries
- Mechanisms must enable proactive and on-request information sharing
- Practical tools: standardized forms, secure channels, joint teams, shared data platforms

### Group B — Money Laundering and Confiscation (R.3–R.4)

#### R.3 — Money Laundering Offence (Delito de lavado de activos)

Countries must criminalize ML based on the Vienna and Palermo Conventions, covering all serious offences as predicate offences. The designated categories include 21 crime types: organized crime, terrorism, human trafficking, drug trafficking, corruption, fraud, tax crimes, environmental crimes, cybercrime, etc.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- Threshold approach: offences punishable by >1 year imprisonment (or >6 months minimum)
- Intent and knowledge may be inferred from objective factual circumstances
- Criminal liability must apply to both natural and legal persons
- Predicate offences extend to conduct occurring in another country

#### R.4 — Confiscation and Provisional Measures (Decomiso y medidas provisionales)

Countries must prioritize asset recovery both domestically and internationally. Competent authorities must be able to: identify, trace, and value criminal property; freeze/seize expeditiously; confiscate (conviction-based and non-conviction-based); execute confiscation orders; and manage frozen/seized/confiscated property effectively.

**Interpretive Note highlights (Oct 2023 revision):**

- Countries must review their asset recovery systems for ongoing effectiveness
- Provisional measures may be applied ex parte or without prior notice
- Extended confiscation: courts may confiscate other property of convicted persons if satisfied it derives from criminal conduct
- Non-conviction-based confiscation: required where consistent with fundamental legal principles
- Asset recovery funds: countries should consider establishing funds for confiscated assets
- Tax authorities must be able to cooperate with law enforcement on asset recovery

### Group C — Terrorist Financing and Proliferation Financing (R.5–R.8)

#### R.5 — Terrorist Financing Offence (Delito de financiamiento del terrorismo)

Countries must criminalize TF based on the International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism. TF must cover financing of: (a) terrorist acts, (b) terrorist organizations, and (c) individual terrorists — even without a link to a specific act. TF must be a predicate offence for ML. Includes financing of foreign terrorist fighter travel (Oct 2015 revision).

#### R.6 — Targeted Financial Sanctions Related to Terrorism (Sanciones financieras dirigidas — terrorismo)

Countries must implement sanctions regimes for UNSC Resolution 1267 (Al-Qaida/Taliban) and Resolution 1373 (country-designated terrorists). Requirements: freeze without delay, prohibit making funds available, communicate designations to FIs/DNFBPs immediately, provide delisting procedures, handle false positives, allow access for basic expenses.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- "Without delay" means within hours, not days
- Designation standard: "reasonable grounds" or "reasonable basis to suspect or believe"
- Freezing extends to all funds/assets owned or controlled (directly/indirectly), including derived/generated assets
- FIs and DNFBPs must report frozen assets and attempted transactions

#### R.7 — Targeted Financial Sanctions Related to Proliferation (Sanciones financieras dirigidas — proliferación)

Countries must implement UNSC sanctions related to WMD proliferation (DPRK: Resolution 1718 and successors; Iran: Resolution 1737 and successors). Same freezing/prohibition framework as R.6 but focused on proliferation actors.

**Oct 2025 revision:** Updated references for Iran-related UNSC resolutions following re-application.

#### R.8 — Non-Profit Organizations (Organizaciones sin fines de lucro)

Countries must identify NPOs within the FATF definition, assess their TF risks, and apply focused, proportional, risk-based measures — without unduly disrupting legitimate charitable activities. Protection covers: (a) terrorist organizations posing as legitimate entities, (b) exploitation of legitimate entities as conduits, (c) diversion of funds to terrorists.

**Oct 2023 revision:** Strengthened risk-based measures requirement.

### Group D — Preventive Measures (R.9–R.23)

#### R.9 — Financial Institution Secrecy Laws (Leyes sobre el secreto de las instituciones financieras)

Countries must ensure that financial secrecy laws do not impede FATF implementation.

#### R.10 — Customer Due Diligence (Debida diligencia del cliente — DDC)

The cornerstone preventive measure. FIs must conduct CDD when:

- Establishing business relationships
- Conducting occasional transactions above USD/EUR 15,000
- There is suspicion of ML/TF
- There are doubts about previously obtained identification data

**CDD measures (a)–(d):**

- **(a)** Identify the customer and verify identity using reliable, independent documents/data
- **(b)** Identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable steps to verify identity; for legal persons, understand ownership and control structure
- **(c)** Understand the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship
- **(d)** Conduct ongoing due diligence: monitor transactions for consistency with customer knowledge, business, and risk profile; determine source of funds when necessary

**Scope determined by RBA:** The extent of CDD measures must be calibrated using a risk-based approach per R.1.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- Verification may be completed after establishing the relationship if ML/TF risks are managed and it's essential not to interrupt normal business
- If CDD cannot be completed: do not open account, do not begin relationship, do not execute transaction; terminate existing relationship; consider filing STR
- Apply to all new customers AND existing customers (risk-based, at appropriate times)
- Anonymous accounts and obviously fictitious names are prohibited

**Higher risk factors:**

- Customer: non-resident, private companies with nominee shareholders/bearer shares, cash-intensive businesses, unusual/complex ownership structures
- Country: poor AML systems, sanctions/embargoes, high corruption, designated TF-supporting countries
- Product/service: private banking, anonymous transactions, non-face-to-face without mitigation, payments from unknown/unassociated third parties

**Lower risk factors:**

- Customer: supervised FIs/DNFBPs, public companies with disclosure requirements, government entities
- Product: low-premium life insurance, pension schemes, limited financial products for inclusion
- Country: adequate AML systems, low corruption

**Enhanced CDD (EDD):** Examine complex/unusual large transactions, increase monitoring degree and nature, obtain additional information (occupation, assets, source of funds/wealth, reasons for transactions), senior management approval, more intense monitoring

**Simplified CDD:** Verify identity after establishing relationship (above thresholds), reduce update frequency, reduce monitoring, infer purpose from transaction type — never allowed when suspicion exists

**Thresholds:** USD/EUR 15,000 for occasional transactions (includes linked transactions)

#### R.11 — Record Keeping (Mantenimiento de registros)

FIs must maintain all transaction records for at least 5 years (sufficient for transaction reconstruction). CDD records (copies of ID documents, account files, business correspondence, analysis results) must be kept for at least 5 years after the relationship ends or the occasional transaction date. Records must be available to competent authorities.

#### R.12 — Politically Exposed Persons (Personas expuestas políticamente — PEP)

For **foreign PEPs** (customer or beneficial owner): (a) risk management systems to determine PEP status, (b) senior management approval, (c) source of wealth and funds, (d) enhanced ongoing monitoring.

For **domestic PEPs** and persons with prominent functions in international organizations: reasonable steps to determine PEP status; if higher risk, apply (b), (c), (d).

Requirements extend to **family members and close associates** of all PEP types.

Life insurance: determine if beneficiaries are PEPs at latest at time of payout.

#### R.13 — Correspondent Banking (Banca corresponsal)

For cross-border correspondent banking relationships, FIs must: (a) gather sufficient information about the respondent institution, (b) assess its AML/CFT controls, (c) obtain senior management approval, (d) understand respective responsibilities, (e) for payable-through accounts, ensure the respondent has performed CDD on its direct-access customers. Shell banks are prohibited.

#### R.14 — Money or Value Transfer Services (Servicios de transferencia de dinero o valores — STDV)

MVTS providers must be licensed/registered and subject to effective monitoring. Agents must also be licensed/registered or maintained on an accessible list.

#### R.15 — New Technologies (Nuevas tecnologías)

Countries and FIs must identify and assess ML/TF risks from new products, practices, and technologies — before launch for FIs. Virtual asset service providers (VASPs/PSAVs) must be regulated, licensed or registered, and supervised by a competent authority (not an SRB).

**Interpretive Note highlights (Jun 2019, revised Jun 2021):**

- Virtual assets = "property", "proceeds", "funds", "funds or other assets", or "equivalent value" for all FATF Recommendations
- CDD threshold for VASPs: USD/EUR 1,000 (vs. 15,000 for traditional FIs)
- Travel rule (R.16) applies to virtual asset transfers: originator VASP must obtain/maintain/transmit originator and beneficiary information
- VASPs must be supervised by a competent authority based on risk
- International cooperation: VASP supervisors must exchange information with foreign counterparts regardless of nature/status differences

#### R.16 — Payment Transparency (Transparencia de pagos)

**Jun 2025 major revision:** FIs must include required originator and beneficiary information in payments/transfers throughout the payment chain, structured per applicable messaging standards (e.g., ISO 20022).

**Cross-border transfers above threshold (USD/EUR 1,000):**

- Originator: name, account number, address, date of birth (natural persons); BIC/LEI/official ID (legal persons)
- Beneficiary: name, account number, country/city name; BIC/LEI/official ID (legal persons)

**Cross-border transfers below threshold:** Name + account number of originator and beneficiary (no verification required unless suspicion)

**Domestic transfers:** Same originator information as cross-border (above threshold) or may be transmitted by other means within 3 business days of request.

**Exceptions:** Inter-FI settlements, net settlements, batch transfers (with full info in the batch), card payments (card number sufficient).

**Monitoring:** FIs must monitor for transfers lacking required information and take appropriate measures. FIs must take freezing measures and prohibit transactions with designated persons/entities.

#### R.17 — Reliance on Third Parties (Dependencia en terceros)

Countries may allow FIs to rely on third parties for CDD elements (a)–(c), provided: (a) immediate access to necessary information, (b) copies of ID data available on request without delay, (c) the third party is regulated/supervised for CDD and record keeping, (d) country risk of the third party is considered. Within the same financial group, conditions (b) and (c) may be met through group-level programs.

#### R.18 — Internal Controls, Foreign Branches and Subsidiaries (Controles internos y filiales y subsidiarias)

FIs must implement AML/CFT programs. Financial groups must implement group-wide programs including policies and procedures for information sharing. Foreign branches and majority-owned subsidiaries must apply home-country AML/CFT measures through group-level programs.

**Interpretive Note highlights (Nov 2017 revision):**

- Programs must include: compliance management, screening procedures, ongoing training, independent audit
- Information sharing within groups about suspicious/unusual transactions
- Branches/subsidiaries must inform the group of host-country restrictions that prevent proper implementation

#### R.19 — Higher-Risk Countries (Países de mayor riesgo)

FIs must apply EDD to relationships and transactions with persons from countries for which FATF calls for such measures. Countries must be able to apply countermeasures when FATF calls for them, and independently.

#### R.20 — Suspicious Transaction Reporting (Reporte de operaciones sospechosas — ROS)

If an FI suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are proceeds of criminal activity or related to TF, it must promptly report to the FIU. This must be required by law.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- Applies to attempted transactions as well
- All suspicious transactions must be reported regardless of amount
- FIs should report promptly — attempted transactions and transactions where CDD could not be completed should also be considered

#### R.21 — Tipping-Off and Confidentiality (Revelación y confidencialidad)

FIs, directors, officers, and employees must be (a) protected from criminal/civil liability for good-faith STR filing, and (b) prohibited from disclosing that an STR is being filed. These provisions must not inhibit R.18 information sharing.

#### R.22 — DNFBPs: Customer Due Diligence (APNFD: Debida diligencia del cliente)

CDD requirements of R.10, R.11, R.12, R.15, and R.17 apply to DNFBPs in specific situations:

- **Casinos:** financial transactions at or above the applicable threshold
- **Real estate agents:** purchase/sale transactions for clients
- **Precious metals/stones dealers:** cash transactions at or above threshold
- **Lawyers, notaries, accountants:** when conducting specified transactions (real estate, money/securities/assets management, bank accounts, company formation/management)
- **Trust and company service providers (TCSPs):** all specified activities

#### R.23 — DNFBPs: Other Measures (APNFD: Otras medidas)

R.18–R.21 apply to all DNFBPs. Specific STR reporting obligations for lawyers/accountants, precious metals/stones dealers, and TCSPs in their respective activities.

### Group E — Transparency and Beneficial Ownership (R.24–R.25)

#### R.24 — Transparency and Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons (Transparencia y beneficiario final de personas jurídicas)

**Mar 2022 revision:** Countries must assess risks of misuse of legal persons, ensure adequate/accurate/up-to-date beneficial ownership information accessible to competent authorities quickly and efficiently — through a **beneficial ownership registry or alternative mechanism**. No new bearer shares or bearer share warrants. Measures against misuse of nominee shareholders/directors. Countries should consider facilitating BO access for FIs and DNFBPs.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- BO definition: the natural person(s) who ultimately own(s) or control(s) a customer, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted; includes persons exercising ultimate effective control
- Multiple natural persons may be BO of a single legal person
- Countries must ensure mechanisms to verify BO information accuracy
- Sanctions for non-compliance/provision of inaccurate information

#### R.25 — Transparency and Beneficial Ownership of Legal Arrangements (Transparencia y beneficiario final de estructuras jurídicas)

**Feb 2023 revision:** Countries must assess risks and ensure adequate/accurate/up-to-date information on express trusts and similar legal arrangements — including settlor(s), trustee(s), protector(s), beneficiaries (or class), and any natural person exercising ultimate effective control. When the trustee or other party is a legal person, its own BO must be identified.

### Group F — Powers and Responsibilities of Competent Authorities (R.26–R.35)

#### R.26 — Regulation and Supervision of Financial Institutions (Regulación y supervisión de instituciones financieras)

FIs must be subject to adequate regulation and supervision implementing FATF Recommendations effectively. Competent authorities must prevent criminals from holding significant/controlling interests or management functions. No shell banks. Core Principles institutions: consolidated group supervision for AML/CFT. Other FIs: licensed/registered and supervised for AML/CFT on a risk basis.

#### R.27 — Powers of Supervisors (Facultades de los supervisores)

Supervisors must have adequate powers to: supervise/monitor, conduct inspections, compel information production, and impose sanctions (disciplinary and financial, including license withdrawal/restriction/suspension).

#### R.28 — Regulation and Supervision of DNFBPs (Regulación y supervisión de las APNFD)

Casinos: comprehensive regulation/supervision regime, licensing, fit-and-proper requirements. Other DNFBPs: effective systems for AML/CFT monitoring/compliance, either by a supervisor or an appropriate SRB. Fit-and-proper requirements and effective sanctions.

#### R.29 — Financial Intelligence Units (Unidades de inteligencia financiera — UIF)

Countries must establish an FIU as the national center for: (a) receiving and analyzing STRs, and (b) other ML/TF-relevant information, and for disseminating results. The FIU must be able to obtain additional information from reporting entities and have timely access to financial, administrative, and law enforcement information.

#### R.30 — Responsibilities of Law Enforcement and Investigative Authorities

Designated authorities must be responsible for ML/TF investigations within national AML/CFT policies. Proactive parallel financial investigations in all cases involving substantial proceeds. Must identify, trace, and initiate freezing/seizure of criminal property. Multi-disciplinary teams and cooperative investigations with foreign authorities.

#### R.31 — Powers of Law Enforcement and Investigative Authorities

Competent authorities must be able to access all necessary documents and information, search persons/premises, take witness statements, seize evidence. Must use a wide range of investigative techniques: undercover operations, communication interception, computer system access, controlled deliveries. Timely access to asset registries, tax information, citizenship records. Mechanisms to identify accounts without prior notice to the owner.

#### R.32 — Cash Couriers (Transporte de efectivo)

Countries must detect physical cross-border transport of currency and bearer negotiable instruments through a declaration and/or disclosure system. Authority to stop/restrain on suspicion. Sanctions for false declaration. Confiscation where related to ML/TF.

#### R.33 — Statistics (Estadísticas)

Countries must maintain comprehensive statistics on: STRs received/disseminated, ML/TF investigations/prosecutions/convictions, property frozen/seized/confiscated, mutual legal assistance and other international cooperation requests.

#### R.34 — Guidance and Feedback (Guía y retroalimentación)

Competent authorities, supervisors, and SRBs must establish guidelines and provide feedback to help FIs and DNFBPs apply national AML/CFT measures, particularly for detecting and reporting suspicious transactions.

#### R.35 — Sanctions (Sanciones)

Countries must ensure a range of effective, proportionate, and dissuasive sanctions (criminal, civil, or administrative) for persons covered by R.6 and R.8–R.23 who fail to comply with AML/CFT requirements. Sanctions must be applicable to FIs, DNFBPs, their directors, and senior management.

### Group G — International Cooperation (R.36–R.40)

#### R.36 — International Instruments (Instrumentos internacionales)

Countries must ratify and implement: Vienna Convention (1988), Palermo Convention (2000), UN Convention against Corruption (2003), and the International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism (1999).

#### R.37 — Mutual Legal Assistance (Asistencia legal mutua)

Countries must provide rapid, constructive, and effective MLA for ML/TF investigations and proceedings. No refusal based solely on: fiscal matters, financial secrecy laws, or pending domestic proceedings. Clear and efficient processes with a case management system.

#### R.38 — MLA: Freezing and Confiscation (Asistencia legal mutua: congelamiento y decomiso)

Countries must take rapid action on foreign requests for identification, tracing, evaluation, investigation, freezing, seizure, and confiscation of criminal property. Must recognize and enforce foreign freezing/confiscation orders. Must manage property subject to confiscation and share/return confiscated assets.

#### R.39 — Extradition (Extradición)

Countries must execute extradition requests for ML/TF constructively and effectively. ML and TF must be extraditable offences. Simplified extradition mechanisms encouraged.

#### R.40 — Other Forms of International Cooperation (Otras formas de cooperación internacional)

Competent authorities must provide the widest range of international cooperation rapidly. Applies to FIUs, financial supervisors, and law enforcement. Information exchanged must be used only for the purpose requested (purpose limitation), with prior authorization for other uses. Safeguards for confidentiality.

**Interpretive Note highlights:**

- FIUs must exchange information regardless of respective status (administrative, law enforcement, judicial)
- Financial supervisors must cooperate regardless of nature/status, exchanging regulatory, prudential, and AML/CFT information
- Law enforcement must share information for intelligence/investigation, create joint investigation teams, use bilateral/multilateral networks
- Spontaneous sharing of information on criminal property is encouraged
- Countries must participate in Asset Recovery Inter-agency Networks (ARINs)

## 3. Key Concepts

### 3.1 Risk-Based Approach (EBR — Enfoque Basado en Riesgo)

The foundational principle: measures must be **proportional** to the level of risk identified. Higher risk demands enhanced measures; lower risk allows (and must be encouraged to allow) simplified measures. Applies at both the country level (national risk assessments) and the entity level (institutional risk assessments).

**Feb 2025 revision** significantly strengthened the proportionality requirement — countries must not only _permit_ but actively _encourage_ simplified measures in lower-risk areas.

### 3.2 Customer Due Diligence (DDC/CDD — Debida Diligencia del Cliente)

The four pillars: (a) customer identification and verification, (b) beneficial owner identification and verification, (c) understanding purpose/nature of the relationship, (d) ongoing monitoring. The scope of each pillar is calibrated by risk.

### 3.3 Beneficial Ownership (Beneficiario Final)

The natural person(s) who ultimately own(s) or control(s) a legal person or arrangement. For legal persons: those with ownership/control interests. For trusts: settlor, trustee(s), protector, beneficiaries, and any person exercising effective final control. Only natural persons can be beneficial owners.

### 3.4 Politically Exposed Persons (PEP — Personas Expuestas Políticamente)

Three categories: (1) foreign PEPs (mandatory EDD), (2) domestic PEPs (EDD when higher risk), (3) persons with prominent functions in international organizations (EDD when higher risk). Extends to family members and close associates.

### 3.5 Suspicious Transaction Reporting (ROS — Reporte de Operaciones Sospechosas)

FIs must report to the FIU promptly when suspicion exists or there are reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are proceeds of crime or related to TF. No minimum threshold. Includes attempted transactions.

### 3.6 Targeted Financial Sanctions (SFD — Sanciones Financieras Dirigidas)

Two regimes: (1) terrorism-related (R.6) covering UNSC 1267 and 1373, (2) proliferation-related (R.7) covering UNSC 1718 (DPRK) and 1737 (Iran). Both require freezing without delay and prohibiting making funds available.

### 3.7 Virtual Assets and VASPs (Activos Virtuales y PSAV)

Virtual assets are digital representations of value that can be digitally traded/transferred and used for payments or investments. VASPs must be licensed/registered, subject to AML/CFT supervision by competent authorities (not SRBs). CDD threshold: USD/EUR 1,000. Travel rule applies.

### 3.8 DNFBPs (APNFD — Actividades y Profesiones No Financieras Designadas)

Six categories: casinos, real estate agents, precious metals/stones dealers, lawyers/notaries/independent legal professionals, accountants, trust and company service providers. Subject to CDD and STR obligations in their respective activities.

## 6. The Evaluation Methodology

### 6.1 Two-Component Assessment

Countries are evaluated through **Mutual Evaluations** (by FATF/FSRBs) or **Third-Party Evaluations** (by IMF/World Bank), using two complementary approaches:

1. **Technical Compliance** — Does the country's legal and institutional framework meet FATF requirements? Assessed against specific criteria for each of the 40 Recommendations.

2. **Effectiveness** — Does the system actually produce results? Assessed against 11 Immediate Outcomes (IOs) that define what a robust AML/CFT/CFP system should achieve.

### 6.2 Risk and Context

Every evaluation starts with understanding:

- Nature and extent of ML/TF risks
- Country circumstances (economy, financial sector composition)
- Structural elements (political stability, rule of law, judicial independence)
- Other contextual factors (corruption, financial exclusion, informal economy)

### 6.3 Materiality and Sector Weighting

Evaluators must weight each sector (banking, insurance, securities, DNFBPs, VASPs, etc.) as **"very important"**, **"moderately important"**, or **"less important"** based on risk, materiality, and context. Higher-weighted sectors carry more influence on ratings.

### 6.4 Technical Compliance Ratings

Each Recommendation is rated:

- **Compliant (C)** — No shortcomings
- **Largely Compliant (LC)** — Only minor shortcomings
- **Partially Compliant (PC)** — Moderate shortcomings
- **Non-Compliant (NC)** — Major shortcomings
- **Not Applicable (NA)** — Requirement does not apply

### 6.5 Effectiveness Ratings

Each Immediate Outcome is rated:

- **High level of effectiveness (HE)** — Fundamental objectives substantially achieved, minor improvements needed
- **Substantial level of effectiveness (SE)** — Major objectives achieved, moderate improvements needed
- **Moderate level of effectiveness (ME)** — Some objectives achieved, major improvements needed
- **Low level of effectiveness (LE)** — Objectives not achieved or achieved to a negligible extent, fundamental improvements needed
